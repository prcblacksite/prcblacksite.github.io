## What this actually is {#what}

PRC is a roleplay group on Roblox. You play somebody who works at a secret site on an island. A government-funded company holds the contract, and the place is being built from nothing.

**The site is not finished. That is the whole point.** Most groups in this genre hand you a finished facility and you walk the same corridors forever. Here the map is the scoreboard.

{{ loop }}

On day one there is a supply pier, a detainee camp, an open excavation called **the Cut**, and an adit driven into the eastern mountain. Everything else on the plan is scheduled, funded and not yet built.

## Branches and ranks are two different things {#roles}

This trips up almost everyone, and it's simple once the two words are separated. You hold **one of each**.

- **Your branch is what you do:** which department you are in.
- **Your rank is how far up you are:** six steps, the same six in every department.

Rank names come in three layers, and you can ignore two of them for a long time:

{{ rank-layers }}

> **The short version:** your branch is your job, your rank is how far up you are, and the grade is just a label so two people with different job titles know they are equals.

### How you move up

Three things, and you need all three or it doesn't happen: **time in grade**, **a number somebody can point at**, and **a sponsor** who puts their name on it. Promotions are posted publicly with the numbers attached, and nobody signs their own. More in [Moving up](/wiki/promotion/).

### Clearance, briefly

Clearance follows from your rank automatically. There is no application for it, which is why there is no channel for requesting it.

## Starting in the camp is not starting at the bottom {#camp}

Detainee Labour takes anyone, instantly, with no application. That sounds like the consolation prize and it isn't. The camp has its own ladder, its own economy and its own way out.

{{ camp-ladder }}

> You can start as a prisoner with a number and end up a Security sergeant. A fair number of the staff will have come up exactly that way.

## The week {#week}

Two scheduled operations, same days, same time, every week. Everything else is whenever you feel like logging in.

{{ week }}

## The works programme {#works}

The number the whole group plays against. It is posted every morning whether it moved or not, because a number that didn't move is a reason to log in.

{{ works-example }}

- **Digging** raises the tonnage, which raises the bar.
- **Building** spends the budget and places a module on the map, permanently.
- **A raid** steals material and drops the bar. On purpose: it gives the next works shift a reason to exist.
- **The bar filling** opens a new part of the site for everybody, forever.

When a ship is due, `#manifest-vote` gets three options for what it carries and the whole server votes. And there is a line on the board marked **Drift**. It goes up. Nobody will explain it yet.

## How we treat each other {#conduct}

> **Everyone here is a real person. How you treat them matters regardless of what either of you is pretending to be in-game.**

In-character conflict is the game working. None of it is a reason to be unkind to the actual human on the other end. Anyone can say "out of character" and the scene stops, with no questions and no exception for rank. The full standard is on [Conduct](/wiki/conduct/).
