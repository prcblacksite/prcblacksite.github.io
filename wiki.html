What PRC is

Every other group in this genre hands you a site that already exists. This one makes you dig it out of the ground, and the map remembers.

The short version

It's an SCP-genre roleplay group, but the site isn't built yet. A government blacksite on an island. Detainees dig the site out of the mountain, Facilities build with what gets dug, Security runs the camp, and the Mobile Vanguard Force handles it when something lands. Everything that gets built stays on the map permanently, so the site you play in next month is the one people actually dug.

The longer version

PRC is Prometheus Research Corporation. It is government funded and it is not the Foundation. The Foundation contains things. PRC contains domain failures: places, processes and systems that stopped behaving.

The site is on a fall-themed island, and at launch it barely exists. There is a supply pier, an intake hut, a prisoner camp, a checkpoint, an open dig called the Cut, and one corridor inside the mountain with three doors that don't open yet.

There's a community progress bar. When it fills, the next part of the site unlocks, and it unlocks for everybody, permanently. Floor 2 does not exist until people dig it. In every other group the site is scenery. Here the map is the scoreboard.

What a domain failure is

The Foundation's unit of work is an object: a thing, in a box, with a number and a containment procedure. PRC's unit of work is a place that stopped obeying itself. A mine where the rock does not stay where it was put. A process that produces a different output than its inputs allow. A building whose floor count disagrees with its stairwell.

You do not contain a domain failure by locking a door. You contain it by occupying the ground, surveying it, building on it, and holding it. That is a construction project, which is why the game is one. It is also why there are prisoners: this is survey and excavation work in a place that is not reliably lawful, so the state supplies the labour and the Corporation is contracted to run it.

The charter, in the site's own voice

PROMETHEUS RESEARCH CORPORATION
SITE-███ · RISK MITIGATION · UNIT-Δ

The Corporation is funded by government contract to identify, occupy and stabilise domain failures: locations, systems and processes that have ceased to behave according to their own rules. We do not collect anomalies. We move onto them and we do not leave.

Site-███ is a contract site. It consists of a supply pier, a detainee camp, an open excavation designated the Cut, and an adit driven into the eastern massif. Everything else on the licence is scheduled, funded, and not yet built.

Detainee Labour digs. Facilities Maintenance builds what is dug. Logistics moves what is built. Security Division counts them out and counts them back. Mobile Vanguard Force answers for the rest of it.

Personnel are reminded that the works programme is the site. What is built here is built permanently and is not subject to reversal by individual request.

Something below the fourth floor has been here longer than any of us. We are all still figuring out what that means.

The game

The loop

Dig, carry, build, and the map changes. Everything else in PRC exists to keep that turning.

The Cut, step by step

  1. A detainee works a rock face with a pickaxe.
  2. Every few swings, spoil drops into their hands. They can carry two at most.
  3. Rarely, a swing hits a pocket and gives double. Very rarely, it turns up a find.
  4. They carry it to the skip. The walk is the gameplay, and it is what Security escorts.
  5. Dropping it in the skip pays credits to whoever carried it and adds tonnes to the site's budget.
  6. As the tonnes add up, the Cut gets visibly deeper.

The truck

One truck. It carries supplies, not spoil: from the pier where the ship unloads, up to the stores. A Logistics Storeman drives it.

Building

Building is not freeform. Around the site are module slots, each showing a ghost outline of what goes there. A Facilities member at a slot sees the cost in budget and supplies. If both are there, the build starts: a few minutes of Facilities members working at the slot under scaffolding, and then the real module appears. It is on the map for everyone from then on.

The bar

The bar is the modules placed. When it reaches a line, the next area of the site opens: a barrier comes down, a door opens, somewhere new becomes reachable. It opens for everybody and it stays open.

Departments and ranks

Ranks

Six steps, the same six in every department, so a Foreman and a Sergeant are the same level and nobody argues about who is in charge.

There are two things true about every member: what they do, which is their department, and how far they have got, which is their grade. The grade carries the authority. The title is the costume: G3 means "runs a shift and the people on it" everywhere, and it is called Sergeant in Security, Foreman in Facilities and Records Supervisor in Administrative.

Command

Above the ladder are three Command seats: Overseer, Continuum Director and Observer. They are appointments, not promotions, and no promotion reaches them.

Clearance

Clearance is not a ladder and there is no application for it. It follows your grade: CL-1 is everyone, CL-2 is G3 and up, and CL-3 is appointed and cannot be reached by ranking up.

Contingent Asset

Everyone is a Contingent Asset from the moment they accept the rules, and keeps it for good. The Chief of Security is still, on paper, a contingent asset.

Seats

From G3 up, a rank is a seat. A department has one G3 seat for every five of its posted members, with a minimum of one, and one G4 seat until it passes twenty. You are promoted into a seat when one is open. That keeps most people at G1 and G2, and it means a supervisor is someone the department actually needs.

When a seat comes free, it is announced in the server, and you can ask to be pinged for it.

How a promotion is decided

  • Three things, every time: time in grade, something countable you actually did, and someone above you putting their name on it.
  • In public. There is a promotion board every Sunday, in a channel, with the numbers attached and the outcome posted.
  • Signed. The higher grades need two signatures, and every promotion is logged with who signed it.
  • Rank is not for sale, trade or favour. Not for Robux, not for art, not for a friend.
  • Rank is not the only reward. Most recognition is a duty role, a commendation on your service record, or a named job.

If you go quiet

Dormancy is not a punishment. Thirty days without activity moves you down one grade and onto the Reserve list. Sixty days returns you to G1. Come back and say so, and you are restored at your old grade with no board and no fuss.

If you are demoted

A demotion always comes with a written reason, and you always see it. You get one appeal per action, to someone who was not involved, answered within seven days.

The game

The camp

Everyone starts here. The camp is the tutorial, and the only thing to learn is the dig.

You step off the boat, you are processed in about a minute, and you are holding a tool. Detainee Labour is a real department with a real ladder, and a detainee can get somewhere without stopping being a detainee.

In the fiction, a release is a sentence discharged and a contract converted: the Corporation offers site employment to discharged detainees because the licence makes leaving the island difficult.

Who runs it

A small staff administers the camp: the Superintendent of Labour sets the work programme and chairs release boards, the Labour Overseer assigns gangs to faces and signs off Trusties, and the Muster Clerk takes the roll and logs output. Security guards it. They are not the same people.

Six people is a site

Six players online on a quiet Tuesday is a working roster: a Duty Officer, a Gate NCO, a Dig Foreman, a Skip Master, and two detainees working a face. The loop turns, the ledger moves, the bar goes up by a little, and nobody is standing in an empty camp wondering where everyone is.

What the Saturday slot rotates through

  • Full shift. Everyone at once, the ordinary thing done properly. This is where a community bonds.
  • Raid. A landing at the pier or the Cut. Material gets stolen and the bar drops, which gives the next works shift a reason to exist.
  • Riot. Starts in the camp. Security holds, the Vanguard resolves. The detainees get to be the threat for once.
  • Selection. Vanguard selection, run in public, so the whole group watches people earn it.
  • Inspection. A quiet one. Photographs, documents, a walk round what got built.
  • The find. A face opens onto something. This one is rare.

Joining

Three steps, and the first two take a couple of minutes.

  1. Join the server at discord.gg/RjzQN8kCPW and accept the rules. The camp channel opens at once.
  2. Link your Roblox account. In #verify, type /verify and follow it. The rest of the server opens when the link is there. Join the Roblox group as well.
  3. Apply to a department with /apply when you know what you want. There is no rush: the camp needs no application.

The application

Six questions, about two minutes:

  • Your Roblox username.
  • Which department, and why that one. One or two lines, not an essay.
  • Roughly how many hours a week, honestly. Two hours honestly beats ten hours optimistically.
  • Whether you can make the Wednesday or Saturday evening ops.
  • Whether you have been in a group like this before.
  • Anything we should know.

Some departments ask for one more thing:

  • Administrative: two paragraphs, an incident report from a prompt, written in the site's voice.
  • Engineering: one screenshot of something you built.
  • Security: a three minute call on procedure, not on lore.
  • Vanguard: no application. See departments.

Every application is answered within 24 hours, including the ones that are turned down.

After you are accepted

You start at G0, on trial, and your department's channel opens. Someone says hello in it. Your first step up is a short procedure check and one supervised shift. See moving up.

If you are not sure

Say what you would rather be doing on an average evening in #faq-desk, and somebody will match you to a department. That works far better than reading the list again.

Conduct

The story is meant to be uncomfortable. Being here isn't.

Every person in this community is a real person. The way you treat them matters regardless of what either of you is pretending to be in game.

Conflict in character is part of the game. Security arrests detainees. The site gets raided. Command makes decisions that upset people. All of that is correct and expected. None of it is a reason to be unkind to the actual human being on the other side of the screen.

The standard

  • Experience is not rank. New members do not know things yet. That is normal, and treating it as a failure is not acceptable.
  • People play differently. Some are here for deep roleplay, some for the action, some to hunt lore. All are valid.
  • Tone matters in text. If someone tells you your tone is causing a problem, take it seriously rather than defending the intent.
  • Staff are held to the same process as everyone else, and do not use rank to settle an argument out of character.

The lines

  • Keep it fictional and non-graphic. No real-world atrocity references and no depiction of torture.
  • No slurs, no real-world politics, no sexual content, nothing that sexualises anyone.
  • Report, don't retaliate.

Leaving a scene

You can always leave a scene. Say you're stepping out, and go. No questions, no consequence, and nobody follows you to finish it.

If someone carries on after you've left, or you're not comfortable with how a scene is being played, tell staff. Staff can pause any scene.

If something goes wrong

Most things are settled by talking. If the behaviour was severe, targeted or clearly intentional, skip that and report it straight away. A good report says what happened, specifically, when it happened, includes screenshots if you have them, and says what outcome you are looking for.

The moderation ladder is warning, timeout, role removal, removal from the group, and ban. Every action is logged with a reason, and you can always ask what is on your own record. You get one appeal per action, to someone who was not involved, answered within seven days.

Complaints about how staff used their powers are taken privately, answered, and counted in public every month.
